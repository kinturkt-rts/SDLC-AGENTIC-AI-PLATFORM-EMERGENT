Apartment Maintenance Desk — tenants report issues; property staff triage and close the loop

Client story:
Jordan manages three apartment buildings. Tenants text photos of leaks and broken appliances;
vendors get verbal instructions; nobody can answer “is unit 4B’s oven still open?” without
digging through messages. He wants a shared maintenance desk: tenants submit requests,
staff prioritize and schedule, and everyone sees status without another group chat.

What the product should do:
- Tenants (or staff on their behalf) submit a maintenance request: building/unit, category
  (plumbing, electrical, appliance, general), urgency (routine vs emergency), description,
  and optional preferred access window.
- Staff triage the queue: acknowledge, set priority, assign to an internal tech or external
  vendor, and schedule a visit window.
- Status that tenants understand: submitted → acknowledged → scheduled → in progress →
  completed / cancelled. Emergency requests should stand out on the queue.
- Capture a short completion note when work is done (and optionally “tenant not home /
  reschedule needed”).
- Building managers see open work by building and aging requests that sat too long.
- Keep a history so disputes (“we never fixed that”) have a timeline.

Who uses it:
- Tenant — submit requests for their unit; see status of their open and recent requests.
- Maintenance coordinator — triage, assign, schedule, cancel, mark complete.
- Building manager — view their buildings’ open work and aging; limited edit (notes / escalate).
- Vendor (optional lightweight) — see jobs assigned to them and mark in progress / done.

Rules that matter in real life:
- Tenants only see and create requests for units they are associated with.
- Emergency should not silently sit in the same pile as routine — surface and age differently.
- Once completed, the request is mostly locked; staff may add a follow-up note if something
  reopens the issue (prefer opening a linked follow-up request rather than rewriting history).
- Cancelling needs a reason (duplicate, tenant withdrew, unable to access, etc.).
- “Too long open” should be obvious for coordinators without exporting a spreadsheet.

Demo data expectations:
- Multiple buildings and units, several tenants.
- A queue with mixed urgencies and statuses, including at least one aging emergency and one
  completed job with notes.
- Assignments to both an internal tech and an external vendor.

Out of scope:
- Rent payments, lease documents, smart locks, full vendor marketplace, photo storage vault
