Wholesale Order Desk — restaurant buyers place produce orders; warehouse confirms and fulfills

Client story:
Leo runs a regional produce wholesaler. Restaurants text or email orders, and his warehouse
team retypes them into a shared sheet. Mistakes are common (wrong case size, cut-off missed,
substitutions forgotten). He wants a calm order desk where buyers place orders before a daily
cut-off, and the warehouse can confirm, substitute, or short-ship with a clear trail.

What the product should do:
- Maintain a catalog of items restaurants can order (name, unit like case or each, pack size,
  and whether the item is available this week).
- Let each restaurant (buyer account) place an order for a delivery day, with line items and
  quantities.
- Enforce a daily order cut-off time; after cut-off, new orders for that delivery day are blocked
  or go into a “late / needs review” pile for staff.
- Warehouse staff review each order: confirm as-is, suggest a substitution when something is
  out, or mark a line as short (couldn’t fill full quantity).
- Buyers see order status: draft → submitted → confirmed → out for delivery → delivered
  (or cancelled).
- Simple order history so a restaurant can reorder last week’s list with edits.
- Staff can see today’s open orders and which ones still need confirmation.

Who uses it:
- Restaurant buyer — browse available items, build and submit orders, see status and history.
- Warehouse clerk — confirm orders, substitutions, short fills, mark out for delivery.
- Wholesale manager — read-only overview of today’s book and problem orders.

Rules that matter in real life:
- Buyers can only order items marked available for the chosen delivery day.
- After submit, buyers should not freely edit line items; they request a cancel or staff adjusts.
- Substitutions need both a reason and the replacement item noted so the restaurant isn’t surprised.
- Short-ships keep the original ask and the filled quantity visible.
- Cut-off is a business rule, not a suggestion — late orders need deliberate staff acceptance.

Demo data expectations:
- A handful of restaurants, a catalog of ~20 items with mixed availability.
- Several orders across statuses for the same delivery day.
- At least one substitution and one short-ship for demo conversations.

Out of scope:
- Online payments, route optimization, inventory counting in the cooler, SMS notifications
