Grant Cycle Tracker for our nonprofit grants team.

Client story:
Priya is the grants lead at a mid-size nonprofit. Opportunities live in email, drafts in
shared drives, and reporting deadlines on sticky notes. They miss renewals and scramble when
a funder asks for a progress report. She wants one place to see every grant from “we might
apply” through “we must report,” with clear owners and due dates — not a full CRM.

Problem:
- Grant opportunities and deadlines are scattered and easy to miss.
- Awarded grants need reporting milestones with owners, but there is no single portfolio view.
- Leadership wants risk and dollars in flight without editing records.

Who uses it:
- Grants coordinator — create and update opportunities, move status, manage reporting tasks.
- Program lead — own specific applications/reports; update notes and mark tasks done.
- Executive director — read-only portfolio and deadline risk view.

MVP features:
- Track grant opportunities: funder, program title, focus area, estimated award size, application deadline.
- Move each opportunity through a simple lifecycle: researching → drafting → submitted → awarded / declined / withdrawn.
- For awarded grants: award amount, start/end dates, and required reporting milestones (what is due, when, who owns it).
- Attach plain notes and checklist items to an opportunity or award (lightweight — not a full document system).
- “Coming due” view: applications due soon and reports overdue or due this month.
- Leadership snapshot: how many in flight, dollars awarded this year, and at-risk deadlines — read-only.
- Seed a mix of opportunities across stages, a few awards with milestones, at least one overdue report and one application due within a week.

Business rules:
- An opportunity should not jump to “awarded” without going through submitted (unless staff record a clear exception note).
- Reporting milestones only exist once a grant is awarded.
- Closing or withdrawing keeps history so the team can learn why they walked away.
- Overdue reports stay highly visible until marked complete.
- Dollar amounts stay simple and editable by coordinators for planning conversations.

Must have for v1:
- Persisted data and clear ownership on applications and reports.
- Deadline and portfolio views that leadership can trust in a meeting.

Nice to have (later):
- AI-written grant narratives, funder portal logins, full budgeting, donor CRM.

Constraints:
- Internal nonprofit use.
- Keep secrets out of source code.

Success:
- Priya opens the tracker, sees what is due this week, updates an opportunity through submitted to awarded, adds a reporting milestone, and leadership sees portfolio risk without editing grants.
