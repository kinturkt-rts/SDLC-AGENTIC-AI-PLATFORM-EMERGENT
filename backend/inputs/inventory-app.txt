Build a simple inventory desk for our small warehouse team.

Problem:
- Stock levels live in spreadsheets and get out of date quickly.
- Staff record sales and restocks in different places, so nobody trusts the numbers.
- The shop manager needs one place to maintain the catalog and see what is running low.

Who uses it:
- The shop manager (admin) maintains categories and products, sets prices, and removes items from the catalog when appropriate.
- Warehouse staff can look up products and record stock changes (sales, restocks, small corrections) but should not restructure the catalog.
- Only signed-in users should access inventory data; health checks for ops can stay open.

MVP features:
- Sign in with username and password. Managers and staff get different permissions.
- Organize products into categories (e.g. Beverages, Snacks, Supplies).
- Each product has a name, SKU, price, and current quantity on hand.
- Staff and managers can adjust stock with a reason (sale, restock, or manual adjustment) and an optional note; every change should leave an audit trail of who did it and when.
- Managers can add, edit, and remove categories and products. Staff can browse and adjust stock only.
- Browse and search products by category, SKU, or name. Highlight items that are low on hand (roughly five units or fewer).
- Managers can delete a product only when its quantity is zero — not while stock remains.

Business rules:
- Quantity should never go below zero.
- Stock quantity changes only through recorded adjustments, not by editing the product directly.
- Inactive users should not be able to sign in.

Must have for v1:
- Persisted data with sensible seed users for demo (at least one manager and one staff account).
- Role-based access so staff cannot create categories or products.
- API suitable for demos (Swagger is fine); no customer-facing web UI required yet.

Nice to have (later):
- Browser or mobile UI, email alerts, barcode scanning, multi-warehouse locations.

Constraints:
- Internal team use only.
- Keep secrets out of source code.

Success:
- Manager signs in, adds a category and product, staff signs in and records a sale, manager sees updated quantity and movement history.
- Low-stock items are easy to find before we run out.
