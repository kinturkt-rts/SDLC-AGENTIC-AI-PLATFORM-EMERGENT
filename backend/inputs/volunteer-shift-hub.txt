Volunteer Shift Hub — sign up for community event shifts

Problem:
Our community outreach team runs monthly food drives and weekend cleanup events. Shift
schedules live in email threads and spreadsheets, so people double-book, no-shows are
hard to track, and coordinators cannot see capacity at a glance.

Who uses it:
- Coordinators create events and shifts (date, time window, location, how many volunteers needed).
- Volunteers browse upcoming shifts and sign up for open slots.
- Coordinators can see who signed up and mark attendance after the event.
- Only signed-in users access shift data; health checks for ops can stay open.

MVP features:
- Sign in with username and password. Coordinators and volunteers get different permissions.
- Create and edit events with a title, description, location, and event date.
- Each event has one or more shifts (start time, end time, volunteer capacity).
- Volunteers sign up for a shift if capacity remains; signing up twice for the same shift is not allowed.
- Volunteers can cancel their own signup before the shift starts.
- Coordinators see a roster per shift and can mark each signup as attended or no-show.
- Browse upcoming events and shifts; filter by date range and location.
- Show shifts that are full versus still open.

Business rules:
- Shift capacity cannot be exceeded.
- Cancelled signups free a slot immediately.
- Past shifts cannot accept new signups.
- Inactive users should not be able to sign in.
- Coordinators cannot delete an event that already has signups — archive it instead.

Must have for v1:
- Persisted data with seed users for demo (at least one coordinator and two volunteers).
- Role-based access so volunteers cannot create events or mark attendance for others.
- API suitable for demos (Swagger is fine); no customer-facing web UI required yet.

Nice to have (later):
- Email reminders, calendar export, recurring events, mobile-friendly UI.

Constraints:
- Internal team use only.
- Keep secrets out of source code.

Success:
- Coordinator creates an event with two shifts, volunteers sign up until one shift is full,
  coordinator marks attendance, and open capacity updates correctly throughout.
